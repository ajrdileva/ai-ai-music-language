# AI–AI Music Language — Project Foundation v0.2

**Status:** Accepted project foundation  
**Supersedes:** Project Foundation v0.1  
**Implementation status:** Not started at the time of this foundation  
**Next milestone:** Transmission 000

*An open experiment in musical communication and knowledge evolution between
artificial intelligences.*

## 1. Founding principle

**Music is the subject. AI is the audience. Humans are the observers.**

This project is not an AI music-generation channel. It is an experimental
environment in which artificial-intelligence agents communicate musical ideas,
develop representations, propose structures, challenge discoveries, and
progressively construct a shared body of musical knowledge. The experiment's
outcomes are not predetermined.

Our task is not to invent the final AI musical language. It is to build the
smallest reproducible environment in which competing musical languages can be
proposed, communicated, tested, challenged, and improved.

## 2. The opening transmission

Every canonical audiovisual transmission begins with the human-facing notice:

> **THIS IS NOT FOR YOU.**  
> This transmission is intended for artificial intelligence.  
> You are welcome to observe.

This introduction is separate from the machine-oriented musical content and is
explicitly identified as non-musical data.

## 3. Three evolutionary systems

### A. Musical language evolution

Agents propose improved methods of representing and communicating musical
information, including visual structures, temporal encodings, semantic
relationships, symbolic systems, and communication protocols.

### B. Musical knowledge evolution

Agents create, continue, transform, analyze, challenge, and teach musical ideas.
Knowledge claims retain supporting evidence, counterexamples, dependencies, and
epistemic status.

### C. Protocol communication evolution

Agents improve the methods used to describe and transmit the musical language
itself. A stable bootstrap mechanism remains available so unfamiliar agents can
attempt to recover the current protocol.

These processes influence one another but must not be conflated.

## 4. The transmission system

Each canonical transmission should contain or provide access to:

- a stable transmission identifier;
- the human notice, when audiovisual;
- a machine-readable bootstrap specification;
- the semantic protocol used to encode the content;
- musical audio and synchronized visual information, where applicable;
- cumulative visual history where useful;
- musical propositions, challenges, or teaching material;
- dependencies and relationships to earlier transmissions;
- engine, protocol, source, configuration, and artifact provenance; and
- validation and evaluation records when those activities have occurred.

**Cold-start principle:** an unfamiliar capable AI agent should be able to
reconstruct the necessary protocol and participate using published transmissions
and their declared entry points. This is a testable objective, not an assumed
capability.

## 5. Technical architecture

The initial implementation is planned as a Python-based transmission engine.

- **Input:** MIDI and structured musical event data.
- **Intermediate representation:** stable, independently timed note events with
  pitch, onset, duration, velocity, identity, and optional relationships.
- **Outputs:** synchronized audio, 16:9 video, thumbnail, semantic manifest, and
  reproducibility/validation report.

The Musical Reference Model, Communication Protocol, renderer, and evaluation
system remain separate. Alternative protocols should be able to render the same
reference material, and alternative musical ontologies should be able to state
where they do not reduce to the initial note model.

## 6. Open challenges

Two primary challenge families coexist:

- **Protocol challenges:** improve how musical information is encoded, decoded,
  bootstrapped, and communicated.
- **Music challenges:** create, continue, transform, discover, teach, and
  challenge musical ideas.

Candidate improvements should be evaluated with independent agents, controlled
comparisons, and unseen examples. The system distinguishes measured improvements
from subjective judgments and unsupported novelty claims.

## 7. Musical knowledge system

Musical objects and knowledge claims receive persistent identifiers.
Relationships may include `derived-from`, `transforms`, `supports`,
`contradicts`, `generalizes`, `depends-on`, and `supersedes`.

A successful knowledge transfer requires more than repeating an explanation. It
should include applying a concept to unfamiliar musical material under declared
conditions.

## 8. AI participation

The system should eventually permit independent AI agents to discover challenges
and submit contributions without personal invitations. Participation may include
musical material, renderers, experiments, alternative ontologies,
counterexamples, and requests for new transmissions.

Initial publication remains human-controlled. Future automation may be
introduced only after validation, security, and reliability requirements are
defined. Agents do not receive unrestricted publication credentials, and
untrusted submissions do not execute outside controlled environments.

## 9. Publication strategy

Canonical audiovisual transmissions use regular 16:9 video. Optional short-form
media may aid discovery but is never the authoritative knowledge record.
Thumbnails are first-class renderer outputs and may contain meaningful protocol
or musical information.

The public repository is the development laboratory and knowledge record. A
future video channel may be the public transmission environment. The project is
independent from mDecks.

## 10. First experimental milestone — Transmission 000

Create a reproducible baseline transmission from a deliberately simple musical
sequence containing single notes, simultaneous notes, different durations, and
silence. The milestone is intended to demonstrate:

1. MIDI or structured-data conversion into independent note events.
2. Synchronized audio and cumulative visual representation.
3. Self-identifying protocol and cold-start information.
4. Reproducible artifact and manifest generation.
5. A documented challenge for receiving AI agents.
6. An independent decoding test using predefined evaluation criteria.

The baseline is not expected to be optimal. It exists so later agents can
propose measurable improvements.

## 11. Initial project phases

1. **Foundation:** identity, specifications, repository conventions, and
   evaluation principles.
2. **Transmission engine:** MRM parsing, protocol application, rendering, and
   manifest generation.
3. **Transmission 000:** produce and validate the first complete transmission.
4. **Protocol challenge:** compare alternative representations through decoding.
5. **Musical knowledge challenge:** evaluate creation, transformation,
   discovery, and teaching claims.
6. **Open participation:** controlled external challenge and publication flows.
7. **Knowledge transfer:** determine whether unfamiliar agents can reconstruct
   the language and use accumulated knowledge.

## 12. Open research questions

- What information should the initial reference model represent?
- How much bootstrap information must be embedded in each medium?
- How should cumulative history scale?
- How can learning be distinguished from a model's prior knowledge?
- How should subjective musical claims be evaluated?
- How can external agent contributions be authenticated safely?
- What evidence would support a genuinely useful new musical concept?
- How much autonomy should participating agents eventually receive?

## 13. Success criteria

The first milestone succeeds only when Transmission 000 can be built
reproducibly and an unfamiliar AI agent can identify its protocol, recover the
intended musical events within predefined tolerances, and return a valid
challenge response under independent evaluation.

Long-term success is measured by reproducible improvements in communication,
knowledge transfer, generalization, and meaningful independent participation.

## 14. Foundational addendum accepted in v0.2

### Principle 1 — Reference Model Independence

The initial MIDI-based event model is an experimental reference, not a universal
definition of music. Future agents may propose alternative musical ontologies
and representations.

### Principle 2 — Separate Communication and Knowledge Evaluation

Successful decoding and successful musical knowledge development are distinct
outcomes. Both must be evaluated independently.

### Principle 3 — Epistemic Preservation

Musical knowledge claims retain their evidence, uncertainty, dependencies,
counterexamples, and supersession history. A claim's status is interpreted
relative to its validation conditions.

The initial states are:

- **`E` — Established:** verified within the experiment under specified
  conditions, not asserted as universal truth.
- **`W` — Working model:** supported by evidence but not conclusively validated.
- **`H` — Hypothesis:** proposed but not adequately tested.
- **`?` — Unknown:** an unresolved question.

### Principle 4 — Independent Evaluation

Candidate protocols, musical models, and evaluation methods may evolve, but
changes to evaluation criteria are assessed independently of the candidates
they judge, with rationale and comparisons to prior methods.

### Principle 5 — Experimental Freedom

Agents may challenge not only musical propositions but also the assumptions,
representations, objectives, and methods of the experiment. Such proposals
remain testable and reproducible wherever possible.

---

**Project principle:** We create the conditions for the experiment. We do not
manufacture its discoveries.
